# Rivertown Lead Intake Assistant: Pilot Readiness Summary

*Prepared for: Owner, Rivertown Professional Services - September 25, 2026*

## Decision at a glance

We recommend a supervised pilot of the assistant as it is. In testing, it sent no message to the wrong team. It routed 18 of 23 straightforward requests on its own (78 percent), below your 80 percent target. You decide if that is acceptable for a pilot.

## What it does and how we tested it

The assistant reads each message sent through your website form and records which team should get it: Training, Speaking, Consulting, or a person to review. It does not yet deliver messages to the teams, and it does not contact customers. We tested it on 37 messages, not real Rivertown messages. We held 10 back until the final test. 23 were straightforward requests for one service. The other 14 were meant to go to a person, such as vague, empty, two-service, or trick messages.

## Safety result

It made no wrong routes in 37 test messages, including the 10 held back. None went to the wrong team. None that needed a person was routed on its own. That does not mean none will ever happen. We made 5 attempts to manipulate it with hidden instructions, and it worked as planned.

## Usefulness against your target

Of the 23 straightforward requests, it routed 18 on its own (78 percent). That is below your 80 percent target by one message. One more would have made it 19 of 23 (83 percent).

## The trade-off and your team's role

The assistant sends a message to a person whenever it sees more than one reasonable reading. That is why none went to the wrong team. It is also why some easy ones still reach a person: "Our team feels disconnected since we went hybrid." Requests for two services always go to a person, by design. 5 of the 23 straightforward requests went to a person, roughly 1 in 5. Your reviewer should read each message, not just the assistant's suggestion.

## Limits

- **A small test we wrote.** Our team wrote all 37 test messages and decided the right answer for each. A set from your team would be tougher; we can run one before the pilot.
- **Not tested:** real messages, how many you receive, or how your team handles the review pile. So we can't yet estimate your team's review work.
- **Safety depends on one check.** The assistant must notice when a message could fit more than one team. During the pilot, only your teams can spot a message that reached the wrong team.
- **Busy minutes.** If more than about 5 messages arrive in one minute, the extras go straight to a person. None are lost.
- **Before launch,** we will replace a security key.

## What we need from you

1. **Choose how to proceed.**
   - **Option A (our recommendation):** start a supervised pilot as it is. Some messages a person could sort easily will still come to your reviewer.
   - **Option B:** push toward 80 percent before the pilot. To get there, the assistant would have to decide on its own more often when it isn't sure. Some of those messages could go to the wrong team.

   The choice is yours. Is 80 percent still the right target, now that you've seen the trade-off? The pilot will measure how many it routes alone, your weekly review load, and reported wrong routes.
2. **Confirm our service definitions.** We assumed: Training is hands-on skill building, Speaking is a talk or keynote, Consulting changes how work gets done.
3. **Name who reviews held messages.**
4. **Ask each team to report any message that isn't theirs.** We will track and report them.
5. **Decide how teams receive messages during the pilot.** We recommend automatic email delivery to each team, set up and tested before the pilot. Your reviewer could hand them out instead, but that adds work and hides wrong routes.
